# Lịch tiêm theo nước (D-084)

Routine childhood schedules outside Việt Nam, read from official pages on the date given and
kept in `packages/contracts/src/vaccine-countries.ts` (the app gets a generated copy). Engineering
drafts awaiting medical review. Below: the sources and what the reviewer should check.

Shared simplifications: ages are whole months (the earliest age of a range); influenza is one row
with the first-season doses and "hằng năm"; vitamin K at birth is listed as "Bệnh viện" where an
official page recommends it (not for Korea, where no official page was found); nasal flu spray (UK)
is stored as an injection row because the schema has only injection and oral.

## US — CDC child immunisation schedule (United States) (checked 2026-10-18)

- CDC — Child and adolescent immunization schedule by age (2025 schedule, addendum July 2, 2025; the version CDC posts on 2026-10-18): https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-age.html
- CDC — Child immunization schedule notes (same version): https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/child-adolescent-notes.html
- CDC — Recommended immunizations for birth through 6 years old (parent version): https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/imz-schedules/child-easyread.html
- CDC — Immunization schedules index (shows the July 2, 2025 schedules as current): https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/hcp/imz-schedules/index.html
- CDC — Hepatitis B immunization fact sheet (December 16, 2025 birth-dose change, later stayed): https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2025/fact-sheet-hepatitis-b-immunization.html
- CDC — January 5, 2026 release on the revised childhood schedule (later stayed): https://www.cdc.gov/media/releases/2026/2026-cdc-acts-on-presidential-memorandum-to-update-childhood-immunization-schedule.html
- CDC — About vitamin K deficiency bleeding (vitamin K shot at birth): https://www.cdc.gov/vitamin-k-deficiency/about/index.html

VERSION USED: the CDC 2025 child schedule (addendum July 2, 2025). On 2026-10-18 this is the schedule CDC's own schedule pages (index, by-age, notes, parent version) present as current; none of them shows a 2026 schedule or a banner. WHY: CDC adopted an ACIP vote on 2025-12-16 making the hepatitis B birth dose an individual decision for babies of HBsAg-negative mothers (start no earlier than 2 months if deferred), and on 2026-01-05 published a narrowed schedule (routine for all only for measles, mumps, rubella, polio, pertussis, tetanus, diphtheria, Hib, pneumococcal, HPV, varicella; hepatitis B, rotavirus, influenza, hepatitis A, meningococcal, RSV, COVID moved to high-risk or shared decision). Per secondary reporting (CIDRAP, Oct 2026; Congressional Research Service IN12684), a federal court (AAP v. Kennedy, D. Mass., preliminary injunction 2026-03-16) stayed the January 2026 schedule and the reconstituted ACIP's votes, including the December 2025 hepatitis B change; the government's appeal was argued at the First Circuit on 2026-10-06 and no ruling was found. I could not open the court order itself (CRS page returned 403), so the legal status is from secondary sources; the CDC pages are what the rows follow. If the First Circuit lifts the stay, hepatitis B, rotavirus, influenza, hepatitis A and RSV may need to move from 'epi' to 'service' and the hepatitis B birth dose may become optional. PROGRAMME: the US has no single free national programme; 'epi' here means 'routine for all children on the CDC schedule' (insurers must cover these without cost-sharing; Vaccines for Children covers eligible children). SIMPLIFICATIONS (earliest recommended age used for ranges): hepatitis B dose 2 at 1-2 months -> 1, dose 3 at 6-18 months -> 6; DTaP dose 4 at 15-18 months -> 15, dose 5 at 4-6 years -> 48; polio dose 3 at 6-18 months -> 6, dose 4 at 4-6 years -> 48; Hib and pneumococcal booster at 12-15 months -> 12; MMR and varicella dose 1 at 12-15 months -> 12, dose 2 at 4-6 years -> 48; hepatitis A 2 doses between 12-23 months at least 6 months apart -> 12 and 18. Rotavirus shows the 3-dose product (2, 4, 6 months); the other product needs only 2 doses (2, 4 months); the series must start before 15 weeks and finish by 8 months. Hib: one product uses only 2 primary doses (2, 4 months) plus the 12-15 month booster. Influenza: first season for children 6 months-8 years is 2 doses 4 weeks apart, then 1 dose every year. RSV: nirsevimab is a monoclonal antibody, not a vaccine; one dose in the first week of life for babies born October-March (just before the RSV season for babies born April-September), not needed if the mother had the RSV vaccine at least 14 days before birth; a second-season dose at 8-19 months is only for high-risk children (left out). A second antibody (clesrovimab) is not mentioned on the CDC page used. LEFT OUT: COVID-19 (shared clinical decision-making for healthy children 6 months-17 years on the CDC page used, not routine; CDC's September 2026 COVID guidance is reported as more restrictive still); meningococcal ACWY and B (high-risk or travel only before age 11; routine ACWY starts at 11-12 years); mpox, dengue, HPV, Tdap (outside the age range or not routine). Vitamin K is not on the vaccine schedule; it is a CDC/AAP recommendation for all newborns (one shot within 6 hours of birth), added as 'hospital' like the VN schedule. The AAP publishes its own schedule (keeps the universal hepatitis B birth dose); not used here because the brief asks for CDC.

## AU — National Immunisation Program (Australia) (checked 2026-10-18)

- Victorian Department of Health — Immunisation schedule Victoria, October 2026 (reproduces the National Immunisation Program schedule): https://www.health.vic.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-10/immunisation-schedule-victoria-october-2026.pdf
- Victorian Department of Health — Immunisation schedule Victoria and vaccine eligibility criteria (updated 1 October 2026): https://www.health.vic.gov.au/immunisation/immunisation-schedule-victoria-and-vaccine-eligibility-criteria
- NSW Health — NSW immunisation schedule (vaccines funded under the NIP as of 1 October 2026): https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/immunisation/app/Pages/schedule.aspx
- Victorian Department of Health — RSV immunisation (nirsevimab infant program 2026, updated 10 July 2026): https://www.health.vic.gov.au/immunisation/respiratory-syncytial-virus-immunisation
- Better Health Channel (Victorian Department of Health) — Vitamin K and newborn babies: https://www.betterhealth.vic.gov.au/health/healthyliving/vitamin-k-and-newborn-babies

VERSION USED: the National Immunisation Program (NIP) schedule in force from 1 October 2026, as reproduced by the Victorian Department of Health schedule (October 2026) and NSW Health (current at 1 October 2026). The Commonwealth pages (health.gov.au NIP schedule page, its September 2026 PDF, and the Australian Immunisation Handbook site) timed out repeatedly from this machine, so the state health department copies were used; both say they mirror the NIP and agree with each other. A reviewer should open https://www.health.gov.au/topics/immunisation/when-to-get-vaccinated/national-immunisation-program-schedule to confirm. AGES: the NIP labels the first visit '2 months (from 6 weeks)'; recorded as 2. Months used: birth 0, 2, 4, 6, 12, 18 months, 4 years = 48. No routine doses at 5-6 years. ROWS AS PARENTS SEE THEM: the 2, 4 and 6 month doses are one 6-in-1 shot (diphtheria, tetanus, whooping cough, polio, hepatitis B, Hib), so hepatitis B has its own row only for the birth dose (same approach as the VN schedule). Boosters are separate shots: DTaP at 18 months, DTaP plus polio at 4 years (one row 'DTaP booster' 18, 48), Hib at 18 months (own row). The 18-month MMR dose is the combined MMRV shot, so it appears in both the MMR row (12, 18) and the varicella row (18). Pneumococcal: 2, 4, 12 months for most children; the extra 6-month dose is only for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children and children with medical risk conditions (notes only). Rotavirus: 2 oral doses; dose 1 must be given by 14 weeks and dose 2 by 24 weeks. Influenza: free every year for children 6 months to under 5 years; first-time recipients aged 6 months to under 2 years (to under 9 with risk conditions) need 2 doses 4 weeks apart. INDIGENOUS / RISK-ONLY ITEMS (notes only, not rows): meningococcal B is NIP-funded only for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander infants (2, 4, 12 months, catch-up under 2) and children with certain medical conditions; extra pneumococcal dose at 6 months; influenza funded from 6 months at any age for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people and people with risk conditions; hepatitis B extras (Victoria). STATE-FUNDED ITEMS: meningococcal B is free for all infants in South Australia (since 2018; SA Health May 2026 release says 6 weeks, 4 and 12 months) and Queensland (6 weeks to under 2 years, from 2025) — from search results, the Queensland page returned 403. I did add a 'service' row for meningococcal B (2, 4, 12 months) because the Australian Immunisation Handbook recommends it for all infants (NSW Health schedule note: 'recommended for other children per the Handbook') and most families outside SA/QLD pay for it privately; delete the row if the owner wants NIP-funded items only. RSV: nirsevimab is a monoclonal antibody, not a vaccine. It is funded by every state and territory (not the NIP) for newborns whose mother did not have the RSV vaccine in pregnancy or had it less than 2 weeks before birth, and for babies with risk conditions; it is usually given before leaving hospital, and some states run it only in season (e.g. Victoria mid-March to 30 September 2026; NSW, ACT, NT year-round per NCIRS). Recorded as 'hospital' at birth; the maternal RSV vaccine (from 28 weeks of pregnancy) is NIP-funded but is given to the mother, not the baby, so it is not a row. A second-season dose at 8-24 months is only for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander and medically at-risk children (notes only). LEFT OUT: COVID-19 (not routinely recommended for healthy children in Australia; NIP lists it only for people with severe immunocompromise and older adults); BCG and travel MMR (Victorian state programs for travel only); hepatitis A (NIP-funded only for Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander children in some states; from prior knowledge, not shown in the sources opened). Vitamin K is not on the NIP; it is recommended for all newborns as one injection at birth (NHMRC joint statement; oral doses are an alternative), added as 'hospital'. The meningococcal B row's 8-week minimum interval is the usual Handbook infant interval from prior knowledge (the Handbook site would not load); please confirm.

## GB — NHS routine childhood vaccination schedule (England) (checked 2026-10-18)

- GOV.UK (UKHSA) — Complete routine immunisation schedule from 1 September 2026: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-complete-routine-immunisation-schedule/complete-routine-immunisation-schedule-from-1-july-2026
- GOV.UK (UKHSA) — The complete routine immunisation schedule (all versions and update history): https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-complete-routine-immunisation-schedule
- NHS — NHS vaccinations and when to have them: https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/nhs-vaccinations-and-when-to-have-them/
- NHS — Child flu vaccine: https://www.nhs.uk/vaccinations/child-flu-vaccine/
- Newcastle upon Tyne Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust — Vitamin K: https://www.newcastle-hospitals.nhs.uk/services/maternity/labour-and-birth/vitamin-k/

Version used: UKHSA 'Complete routine immunisation schedule from 1 September 2026' (gov.uk, updated 24 September 2026; the URL slug still says 'from-1-july-2026'). The July and September 2026 updates only change RSV eligibility (vaccine in pregnancy from 28 weeks, older adults, immunosuppressed people; no infant RSV product is listed), so the childhood rows are the same as the 1 January 2026 schedule. Pregnancy vaccines (RSV, whooping cough, flu) are not modelled. Rows are the schedule for a baby born on or after 1 January 2025: 6-in-1 at 8, 12, 16 weeks and 18 months; rotavirus at 8 and 12 weeks; MenB at 8 and 12 weeks and 1 year (moved from 16 weeks in July 2025); PCV at 16 weeks and 1 year (moved from 12 weeks in July 2025); MMRV at 1 year and 18 months (replaced MMR from 1 January 2026; England had no routine chickenpox vaccine before); 4-in-1 (dTaP/IPV) pre-school booster at 3 years 4 months. The Hib/MenC booster at 1 year is no longer on the schedule. Older cohorts differ and are NOT modelled: children born on or before 30 June 2024 have no 18-month appointment; children born between 1 July 2024 and 31 December 2024 get MMRV at 18 months; children born between 1 September 2022 and 31 December 2024 get their MMRV dose with the pre-school booster at 3 years 4 months. Flu: one row, nasal spray, months [24] with yearly true (same convention as the Việt Nam flu row, which lists only the first-season doses). Routine eligibility is children aged 2 or 3 on 31 August plus all school-aged children, once a year from September; children in clinical risk groups can have an inactivated injection from 6 months, and risk-group children aged 6 months to 8 years having flu vaccine for the first time get a second dose 4 weeks later (not modelled). Schema limitation: route only allows injection or oral, so the nasal flu spray is stored as 'injection' and the about text says it is a nasal spray. Not rows (selective, at-risk only): hepatitis B for babies born to mothers with hepatitis B (monovalent at birth and 4 weeks, then the routine 6-in-1, with a blood test at 12–18 months; babies born on or before 30 June 2024 also had a dose at 12 months); BCG within 28 days of birth for babies in areas with TB incidence of 40 or more per 100,000 or with a parent or grandparent born in a high-TB country (checked against the SCID newborn screening result first); COVID-19 for children from 6 months with a weakened immune system. Vitamin K: offered to all newborns as a single intramuscular injection soon after birth (oral drops in several doses if parents prefer); I could not open nhs.uk or NICE pages for this (404/403), so the source is an NHS trust maternity page. No minIntervalWeeks are set because the schedule tables do not state intervals for the routine rows. The infant PCV on the current table is the 13-serotype vaccine; the PCV20 added in January 2026 is for adults 65+ and clinical risk groups, not the infant schedule. The about text does not name a serotype count.

## SG — National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (Singapore) (checked 2026-10-18)

- CDA — Vaccinations (National Childhood Immunisation Schedule, Table 1): https://www.cda.gov.sg/public/vaccinations/
- CDA — National Childhood Immunisation Schedule, updated 12 October 2026 (PDF): https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/18/34009f97-7100-4624-8eba-89fd5a2b4e3d/NCIS_October%202026.pdf
- MOH — Childhood developmental screening and childhood vaccinations (subsidies): https://www.moh.gov.sg/managing-expenses/schemes-and-subsidies/childhood-developmental-screening-and-childhood-vaccinations/
- Singapore General Hospital — Baby injections (vitamin K, hepatitis B, BCG at birth): https://www.sgh.com.sg/our-specialties/neonatal-and-developmental-medicine/baby-injections

Version used: CDA National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (NCIS) 'Updated 12 October 2026' (table image and 5-page PDF on cda.gov.sg; the October 2026 change adds COVID-19 for children with medical conditions). Birth to 6 years, routine 'recommended for all children' cells only: BCG and monovalent hepatitis B at birth (within 24 hours); 6-in-1 (DTaP-IPV-Hib-HepB) at 2 and 6 months and 5-in-1 (DTaP-IPV-Hib) at 4 and 18 months, which also carry hepatitis B doses 2 and 3 and all four polio doses, so I made one '6in1 / 5in1' row rather than separate DTaP, IPV, Hib and hepatitis B rows; PCV13 at 4 and 6 months with a booster at 12 months; separate MMR and varicella at 12 months and combined MMRV at 15 months (CDA advises separate MMR + varicella for dose 1 because MMRV as a first dose at 12–47 months carries a higher febrile-seizure risk), modelled as three rows (MMR 12, varicella 12, MMRV 15) so each shot is ticked once; influenza for all children 6–59 months, annually or per season, with 2 doses 4 weeks apart the first time (modelled as months [6, 7] with yearly true and minIntervalWeeks 4, the same convention as the Việt Nam flu row). Babies of HBsAg-positive mothers follow a different hepatitis B path (HBIG plus monovalent dose within 12 hours, monovalent at 1 month, 5-in-1 at 2 months, 6-in-1 at 6 months) — not modelled. Not rows (medical condition or indication only): PPSV23 from 2 years, COVID-19 from 6 months (added to the NCIS on 12 October 2026), flu from 5 years. Tdap-IPV booster (10–11 years) and HPV are beyond 72 months. Subsidies: Singapore Citizen children get full subsidy for NCIS vaccines on the Subsidised Vaccine List at CHAS GP clinics; SC and PR children may get up to full subsidy at polyclinics; all rows are therefore 'epi' except the birth injections ('hospital'). Optional vaccines: I found no official Singapore source (CDA, MOH, HealthHub) that recommends rotavirus, MenB, meningococcal ACWY or hepatitis A for all children, so no 'service' rows were added; private clinics do offer them at full cost, but only non-official sites say so. Vitamin K: from the SGH neonatal page (all babies get vitamin K, hepatitis B and BCG before discharge); CDA does not list vitamin K because it is not a vaccine. No brand names in the text.

## JP — Japan routine immunisation schedule (checked 2026-10-18)

- Japan Institute for Health Security (JIHS, formerly NIID) — Routine immunisation schedule and notes, as of 1 August 2026 (ver. 2026.08.01): https://id-info.jihs.go.jp/immunization/schedule/2026/20260801_routine-note_r1.pdf
- Japan Institute for Health Security (JIHS) — Immunisation schedule page (routine and optional vaccines, updated 24 September 2026): https://id-info.jihs.go.jp/immunization/schedule/currently/index.html
- Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare — Vaccination (予防接種) information, standard ages for routine vaccines: https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/kenkou_iryou/kenkou/kekkaku-kansenshou/yobou-sesshu/index.html
- Japan Pediatric Society and 15 societies — Proposal on preventing vitamin K deficiency bleeding in newborns and infants (30 November 2021): https://www.jaog.or.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/20211215.pdf

Ages are the 'standard timing (example)' column of the JIHS/NIID routine schedule ver. 2026.08.01, rounded to whole months; where a range is given the earliest month is used. Simplifications: (1) Hepatitis B: routine programme has no birth dose; the 3rd dose is 'around 7-8 months', 7 used. Babies of HBsAg-positive mothers get a birth dose + HBIG under health insurance (0, 1, 6 months) - not shown. (2) Rotavirus: parents get either the monovalent vaccine with 2 doses (2, 3 months) or the 5-valent with 3 doses (2, 3, 4 months); one row with 3 doses [2,3,4] is shown and the about text says the 2-dose type stops after dose 2. First dose must be by 14 weeks 6 days. (3) 5-in-1 (DTaP-IPV-Hib, routine since April 2024): 2, 3, 4 months, booster at 1 year (standard 6-18 months after dose 3). Older 4-in-1 + separate Hib series not shown. (4) PCV booster 12-15 months -> 12. (5) BCG 5-8 months -> 5. (6) MR phase 2 is 'the year before entering primary school' (age 5-7; Japanese school entry is the April after turning 6), shown as 60 months. (7) Varicella dose 2 is 6-12 months after dose 1 -> 18. (8) Japanese encephalitis phase 1: two doses at age 3 given 6-28 days apart, then dose 3 about a year later at 4; the app needs strictly increasing whole months so dose 2 is shown at 37 months (really 1-4 weeks after dose 1). Phase 2 at 9 years and DT at 11 years are beyond 72 months and omitted. Standard is age 3 but routine eligibility starts at 6 months (some areas, e.g. Hokkaido/high-risk, start earlier). (9) Mumps (optional, 任意接種, paid): JIHS shows 1 dose at 1 year and 1 dose in the year before school -> [12, 60], programme 'service'. (10) Influenza (optional for children, paid; routine only for older adults): 2 doses per season under 13 years, 2-4 weeks apart, from 6 months -> [6, 7] yearly, programme 'service'. Live nasal flu (2-18 years) not shown. (11) Vitamin K: not a vaccine; oral vitamin K2 syrup. The 2021 joint proposal recommends doses at feeding start, at 1 week/discharge, then weekly to 3 months (13 doses); many hospitals still use the 3-dose method (feeding start, 1 week, 1-month check). Shown as [0, 1] hospital/oral. Not included: RSV maternal vaccine (given to the mother in pregnancy), HPV, DT, MenACWY/hepatitis A (travel/high-risk optional). Uncertain: whether the app should show the monovalent rotavirus (2 doses) instead; JE dose 2 month placement.

## KR — South Korea National Immunization Program schedule (checked 2026-10-18)

- Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) — Children's national immunisation standard schedule 2026 (표준예방접종일정표 2026): https://nip.kdca.go.kr/irhp/images/egovframework/rte/infm/Immunization_Schedule_for_Children_2026.jpg
- Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) — National Immunization Program, vaccination information page: https://nip.kdca.go.kr/irhp/infm/goVcntInfo.do?menuLv=1&menuCd=115
- Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency (KDCA) — National Immunization Program (English): https://kdca.go.kr/eng/4408/subview.do

Ages from the KDCA 2026 standard schedule chart (Korean) and the KDCA English NIP page; where a range is given the earliest month is used. All rows are free under the NIP (influenza: free 6 months-13 years). Simplifications: (1) BCG (intradermal) is 'within 4 weeks of birth' -> [0]. (2) DTaP doses 4 (15-18 months) -> 15, dose 5 (4-6 years) -> 48; dose 6 (Tdap/Td at 11-12 years) is beyond 72 months and omitted. (3) Polio dose 3 is 6-18 months -> 6; dose 4 at 4-6 years -> 48. (4) Hib and PCV booster 12-15 months -> 12. PCV/PPSV for high-risk children 2-12 years not shown. (5) Rotavirus: RV1 is 2 doses (2, 4 months), RV5 is 3 doses (2, 4, 6 months); one row with 3 doses [2,4,6] is shown and the about text says the 2-dose type stops after dose 2. (6) Combination vaccines: KDCA offers DTaP-IPV (4-in-1), DTaP-IPV/Hib (5-in-1) and, since January 2025, DTaP-IPV/Hib/HepB (6-in-1) at 2, 4, 6 months. The chart keeps DTaP, polio, Hib and hepatitis B as separate rows, so the app does too; with the 6-in-1 the hepatitis B schedule becomes birth + 2, 4, 6 months (the separate 1-month and 6-month hep B doses are not needed, except for babies of hepatitis B-positive mothers). The app cannot express this choice for KR; reviewer may want a note in the UI. (7) MMR dose 1 at 12-15 months -> 12, dose 2 at 4-6 years -> 48. (8) Varicella: 1 dose at 12-15 months -> 12 (NIP gives one dose). (9) Hepatitis A: 2 doses between 12-35 months, 6+ months apart -> [12, 18]. (10) Japanese encephalitis: NIP offers either inactivated (5 doses: 12-23 months x2 one month apart, 24-35 months, 6 years, 12 years) or live attenuated (2 doses: 12-23 and 24-35 months). One row is shown with the inactivated schedule up to 6 years [12, 13, 24, 72]; the 12-year dose is beyond 72 months. Uncertain which type most families choose; the live vaccine is popular because it needs only 2 doses. (11) Influenza: yearly from 6 months; children under 9 having their first flu vaccine get 2 doses 4 weeks apart -> [6, 7] yearly. (12) Vitamin K: not part of the KDCA NIP and no Korean official page found; a single intramuscular injection at birth is standard hospital practice and is shown as [0] hospital/injection. Not included: HPV (12 years), Td/Tdap (11-12 years). The KDCA web page shows a review date of 2024-01-15 but serves the 2026 chart image.
